## **I. INTRODUCTION**

### **A. Definition**

A Physician Beam Scale is a mechanical measuring device used by medical professionals to measure one’s weight and height manually and accurately. It gives more accurate measurement. The scale consists of a platform, column, and a dual-beam balance system mounted on top.

This type of measuring device does not require electricity, which ensures reliability operation, consistent accuracy, and long term durability. It is valued for their mechanical precision, stability, and ability to provide repeatable measurements in clinical and professional settings.

## **B. Purpose**

The physician beam scale is not only for accurate weight measurement but it is also crucial in healthcare for several reasons. From drug dosage calculations to monitoring growth and development, a person's weight and body mass index (BMI) are critical factors in their medical care and personal well-being. Miscalculating a person's weight can lead to medical errors.

## **C. General Appearance**

Physician beam scales use a mechanical balance system with sliding weights, which is why they are trusted by healthcare professionals for precise weight tracking. The device typically stands between 900 mm and 1,200 mm in height with a capacity of 500lbs and is constructed primarily of enameled steel or aluminum for durability and stability. The slip resistant cast iron base and anti-tip column design also allows patients to be safely measured and weighed. The scale is printed on both sides, allowing both patient and nurse to read the weight. The finish is usually powder-coated in neutral colors (white, gray, or black) to meet clinical hygiene <u>standards.</u>

The overall appearance is functional and professional, characterized by a rectangular base platform, a vertical support column, and a horizontal balance beam assembly mounted at the <u>upper portion of the column.</u>

## II. **DESCRIPTION**

### A. **Main Parts**

### Balance beam

The Balance beam moves up and down depending on the amount of weight placed on the scale.

### Lower Poise Bar

The lower poise bar of the balance beam has large weight incrementations.

## Upper Poise Bar

The upper poise bar of the balance beam has small weight incrementations.

## Large Poise Weight

The large poise weight or large weight indicator moves along the lower poise bar and has an indicator that points to specific weight quantity.

## Small Poise Weight

The small poise weight or small weight indicator moves along the upper poise bar and has an indicator that points to a specific weight quantity.

## Balance Pointer

The balance pointer moves up and down within the balance window.

## Balance Window/ Trig Loop

The balance window is the immovable rectangular shape surrounding the balance pointer. Depending on the amount of weight on the scale, the balance pointer either rises to the top of the window, drops to the bottom of the window, or floats within the middle of the window. The goal is to have the pointer float in the middle of the window.

## Calibration Marks

The calibration marks represent the specific quantities of weight on the scale. This scale measures units of weight in pounds(lb/lbs).

# **B. Sub Parts**

## Column with head

The column is the vertical support structure that connects the platform to the weighing mechanism. The head mounted at the top houses the beam assembly, including the graduated beams, sliding poises, and balance pointer. This component ensures stable alignment and accurate weight measurement.

## Platform base

The platform base is the flat, reinforced surface on which the user stands during weighing. It is designed to provide stability and even weight distribution, minimizing measurement errors. The platform surface is typically non-slip for safety.

## Hand post

The hand post is a vertical or side-mounted support bar that the patient may hold for balance while standing on the scale. It helps prevent shifting or movement, which can interfere with accurate weight readings.

## Height measuring rod

The height measuring rod is an adjustable vertical bar used to measure the patient's height. It typically features a sliding horizontal headpiece that rests on the patient's head to provide an accurate height reading in centimeters or inches.

Hardware kit

The hardware kit includes the screws, bolts, nuts, and assembly tools required to secure the scale components. It ensures that all parts are properly aligned and firmly attached to maintain structural integrity and measurement accuracy.

Wheels

The wheels are typically located at the rear or underside of the scale to allow for easy relocation and positioning within clinical environments. They permit the scale to be tilted and rolled without lifting, while maintaining stability when in use.

## **III. CONCLUSION**

The physician beam scale is a reliable and essential measuring device commonly used in medical and clinical settings. Its general appearance features a stable platform, a vertical column with the beam head, and often an integrated height measuring rod, giving it a sturdy and professional design suitable for repeated daily use. Each part of the scale contributes to its overall function: the platform base supports the patient’s weight, the column with head holds the balancing mechanism, the hand post aids stability, the height measuring rod provides height measurement, the hardware kit ensures secure assembly, and the wheels allow easy movement. Together, these components ensure accurate and consistent weight measurements, making the physician’s beam scale a dependable tool for monitoring health and supporting clinical decision-making.
